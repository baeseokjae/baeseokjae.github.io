Rowboat is an open-source, local-first AI coworker that serves as a direct alternative to Claude Desktop and Claude Cowork. Built by Y Combinator S24 startup Rowboat Labs, it indexes your email, calendar, meetings, and notes into a persistent Obsidian-style knowledge graph, then acts on that context using any LLM you choose — local models via Ollama or LM Studio, or hosted API keys. With 16,704 GitHub stars, Apache-2.0 licensing, and active daily development reaching v0.7.7 as of July 2026, Rowboat is rapidly becoming the most compelling open-source option for knowledge workers who want AI assistance without vendor lock-in or cloud dependency.

What Is Rowboat? — An Overview of the Open-Source AI Coworker#

Rowboat is a desktop application that reimagines how AI assistants interact with your work. Instead of a single chat window where you paste context and get answers, Rowboat provides five integrated work surfaces — email, notes, browser, meetings, and code — all connected through a persistent knowledge graph stored as local Markdown files.

The project was created by Rowboat Labs, a Y Combinator S24 startup whose founders previously built a deep-learning product for enterprise support representatives. The repository was created on January 13, 2025, meaning it has undergone approximately 18 months of active development. As of July 2026, the project has 16,704 stars and 1,660 forks on GitHub, with 121 open issues indicating a healthy, engaged community.

Rowboat hit the front page of Hacker News on July 7, 2026, where it was described as a “local-first alternative to Claude Desktop.” The latest release, v0.7.7, was published on July 20, 2026, demonstrating near-daily development velocity. It is available on Mac, Windows, and Linux.

The Core Differentiator: Knowledge Graph That Compounds#

The single most important feature that sets Rowboat apart from every chat-based AI assistant is its persistent knowledge graph. Traditional AI tools like Claude Desktop or ChatGPT operate on a cold-retrieval model: every conversation starts from scratch, and any context you need must be pasted into the prompt window each time.

Rowboat takes a fundamentally different approach. It continuously indexes your email (Gmail), calendar events, meeting transcripts, and notes into an Obsidian-compatible Markdown vault. This vault becomes a knowledge graph that compounds over time. The more you use Rowboat, the more it knows about your projects, contacts, and workflows.

This matters because context switching is expensive. Research from UC Irvine shows that after an interruption, workers take an average of approximately 23 minutes to return to their original task. Every time you switch from your work to an AI chat window, paste context, and wait for a response, you incur that switching cost. Rowboat eliminates this by keeping your context persistent and accessible across all work surfaces.

The knowledge graph is stored as plain Markdown files in a local vault. This means your knowledge is portable — you can open it in Obsidian, edit it with any text editor, back it up with your existing file sync tools, or migrate it to another system. You are never locked into a proprietary database format.

Five Work Surfaces — Email, Notes, Browser, Meetings, Code#

Rowboat replaces the single-chat-window paradigm with five integrated work surfaces, each designed for a specific type of task.

Email Client with AI Context#

Rowboat includes a built-in email client that connects to Gmail. Because it has access to your knowledge graph, it can draft replies that reference past conversations, project context, and meeting notes without you having to search for them. The AI understands who your contacts are, what projects you’re working on, and what decisions have been made.

Obsidian-Compatible Notes#

The notes surface is an Obsidian-compatible Markdown editor. Every note you write is automatically linked into the knowledge graph. Backlinks between notes are created automatically based on content relationships, just like in Obsidian. This means your notes become a web of interconnected knowledge rather than isolated documents.

Isolated Browser#

Rowboat includes a built-in browser that is isolated from your main browsing session. This is designed for research tasks where you want the AI to help you gather information from the web without mixing with your personal browsing history or cookies. The browser surface can be used alongside the AI assistant, which can read and summarize pages you visit.

Meeting Note-Taker#

The meeting surface connects to your calendar and can take notes during meetings. These notes are automatically indexed into the knowledge graph, linked to the relevant contacts and projects. Over time, this creates a searchable record of every meeting you’ve attended, with all the context preserved.

Code Mode#

Code mode provides a code editing environment with AI assistance. Unlike Claude Desktop’s Artifacts or ChatGPT’s code interpreter, Rowboat’s code mode is designed for ongoing development work. The AI has access to your project context through the knowledge graph, so it understands your codebase structure, dependencies, and conventions.

Local-First Architecture — What It Means and What It Doesn’t#

Rowboat describes itself as “local-first,” which is a distinct concept from “local LLM.” Local-first means your data stays on your machine by default. Your email index, knowledge graph, notes, and configuration are all stored as local files. You control where they live, how they’re backed up, and who has access to them.

However, local-first does not mean everything runs offline. Rowboat can use local models through Ollama or LM Studio for complete offline operation, but it also supports hosted API keys for users who want access to more powerful models. Additionally, some features like voice input and web search call cloud APIs by default. This is an important distinction: your data is local-first, but some functionality may still touch the cloud unless you explicitly configure it otherwise.

The architecture is designed so that you can start with hosted models and gradually move to local models as hardware improves. This flexibility is one of Rowboat’s strongest advantages over Claude Desktop, which forces you into Anthropic’s cloud infrastructure regardless of your preferences.

Installation and Setup Guide — Mac, Windows, Linux#

Rowboat is available for all three major desktop platforms. The installation process is straightforward:

Mac: Download the macOS build from the GitHub releases page or via Homebrew. The application runs as a standard macOS app with menu bar integration.

Windows: Download the Windows installer from GitHub releases. The setup wizard handles dependencies and configuration.

Linux: Available as an AppImage or via package managers. Linux users should note that some features (like the isolated browser) may require additional dependencies depending on the distribution.

After installation, Rowboat walks you through the initial setup: connecting your Gmail account (optional), configuring your LLM provider, and setting up your knowledge graph vault location. The entire process takes approximately 10-15 minutes for a first-time user.

Bring Your Own Model — Ollama, LM Studio, Hosted APIs#

One of Rowboat’s most compelling features is its model-agnostic architecture. You are not locked into any single AI provider. Rowboat supports three categories of models:

Local Models via Ollama: Ollama provides easy access to open-weight models like Llama 3, Mistral, Gemma, and Qwen. These run entirely on your machine, providing complete privacy and offline capability. Performance depends on your hardware — models in the 7B-13B parameter range work well on consumer GPUs, while larger models require more powerful hardware.

Local Models via LM Studio: LM Studio offers a graphical interface for downloading and running GGUF-format models. This is a good option for users who prefer a GUI over Ollama’s command-line interface. LM Studio also supports OpenAI-compatible API endpoints, which Rowboat can connect to.

Hosted API Keys: For users who want access to frontier models without running them locally, Rowboat supports OpenAI, Anthropic, and any OpenAI-compatible API endpoint. This means you can use GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, or any other hosted model while still keeping your knowledge graph and data local.

The ability to switch between providers without changing your workflow is a significant advantage. You can use a local model for routine tasks and a hosted frontier model for complex reasoning, all within the same interface.

MCP Integration — Extending Rowboat with External Tools#

Rowboat supports the Model Context Protocol (MCP), an open standard that allows AI applications to connect with external tools and data sources. Through MCP, Rowboat can integrate with services like Exa (web search), Slack, Linear, GitHub, and ElevenLabs.

MCP is one of several extension mechanisms Rowboat uses. It also integrates with Composio for tool access. This means Rowboat is not limited to its built-in features — the community can build and share MCP servers that extend Rowboat’s capabilities to virtually any service with an API.

For developers, creating an MCP server for Rowboat follows the same standard as MCP servers for Claude Desktop or any other MCP-compatible client. This interoperability means the growing MCP ecosystem of tools and connectors works with Rowboat out of the box.

Background Agents — Automation and Scheduled Tasks#

Rowboat includes a background agent system that can perform tasks on events or schedules. This is a feature that neither Claude Desktop nor Claude Cowork offers.

You can configure agents to:

  • Monitor your email for specific types of messages and draft responses
  • Summarize meetings and add them to your knowledge graph automatically
  • Run scheduled research tasks using the browser surface
  • Trigger workflows based on calendar events
  • Send notifications when specific conditions are met in your knowledge graph

These background agents run locally and respect your data sovereignty. They are configured through Rowboat’s interface and can use any of your configured models.

Privacy and Data Sovereignty — Why It Matters#

For knowledge workers handling sensitive information, the privacy implications of AI tooling are significant. Claude Desktop and Claude Cowork send your prompts and context to Anthropic’s cloud servers. While Anthropic has privacy policies in place, many organizations cannot legally send certain types of data to third-party cloud services due to regulatory requirements (GDPR, HIPAA, SOC 2, internal data classification policies).

Rowboat’s local-first architecture addresses this directly. Your email index, knowledge graph, notes, and configuration never leave your machine unless you explicitly configure a hosted model. Even when using hosted models, your knowledge graph data stays local — only the specific prompt and context needed for a single query are sent to the API.

For organizations evaluating AI tools, this distinction is critical. Rowboat can be deployed in environments where cloud-only AI assistants are not permitted. The Apache-2.0 license also means organizations can audit the code, modify it, and self-host if needed.

Who Is Rowboat For? — Target Users and Use Cases#

Rowboat is designed for knowledge workers who need AI assistance integrated into their daily workflow rather than a separate chat interface. The ideal users include:

Developers and Engineers: Code mode with project-aware context, MCP integration for GitHub and Linear, and background agents for automation make Rowboat a powerful development companion.

Product Managers and Executives: The email client, meeting note-taker, and persistent knowledge graph help PMs track decisions, action items, and project history across multiple teams and initiatives.

Researchers and Analysts: The isolated browser surface combined with the knowledge graph creates a research environment where findings are automatically organized and linked.

Privacy-Conscious Professionals: Anyone who cannot or will not send work data to cloud AI services will find Rowboat’s local-first architecture essential.

Open-Source Advocates: Users who prefer open-source tools and want to avoid vendor lock-in will appreciate Rowboat’s Apache-2.0 license and model-agnostic design.

Limitations and Gotchas — What to Know Before You Install#

While Rowboat is impressive for an open-source project at v0.7.7, it has limitations that potential users should understand:

Beta-Grade Polish: As a v0.7.x release, Rowboat has rough edges. The UI is functional but not as polished as Claude Desktop. Some workflows may require command-line configuration.

Gmail-Only Email: The email surface currently supports Gmail only. Users of Outlook, ProtonMail, or other providers will need to wait for future updates or use workarounds.

Cloud Dependencies in Practice: While Rowboat is local-first, some features (voice, web search) call cloud APIs by default. Achieving a fully offline setup requires careful configuration.

Learning Curve: The five-work-surface paradigm is more powerful than a single chat window, but it takes time to learn. New users may find the interface overwhelming initially.

Active Development Churn: With daily releases, the project changes rapidly. Features may move, configuration options may change, and breaking changes are possible between releases.

Community Support: As an open-source project, support comes from GitHub issues and community discussions rather than a dedicated support team. Response times vary.

Verdict — Is Rowboat the Best Claude Desktop Alternative?#

As of July 2026, Rowboat is the strongest open-source alternative to Claude Desktop and Claude Cowork. Its persistent knowledge graph, five integrated work surfaces, model-agnostic architecture, and local-first design address the fundamental limitations of chat-based AI assistants.

Rowboat is not a drop-in replacement for Claude Desktop — it is a fundamentally different approach to AI-assisted work. Where Claude Desktop is a chat window with artifacts, Rowboat is a complete work environment with persistent context. For users who value data sovereignty, model flexibility, and knowledge that compounds over time, Rowboat is the superior choice.

The project’s active development velocity (v0.7.7 as of July 20, 2026, with near-daily releases), strong community adoption (16,704 GitHub stars), and Y Combinator backing suggest that Rowboat will continue to improve rapidly. For knowledge workers ready to move beyond the chat-window paradigm, Rowboat is worth installing today.

Frequently Asked Questions#

Is Rowboat completely free to use?#

Yes, Rowboat is completely free and open source under the Apache-2.0 license. There are no paid tiers, subscription fees, or hidden costs. You only pay for any hosted AI model APIs you choose to connect, and those costs go directly to the model provider, not to Rowboat Labs.

Can Rowboat work entirely offline?#

Yes, with the right configuration. By using a local model through Ollama or LM Studio and disabling cloud-dependent features like voice and web search, Rowboat can operate entirely offline. All your data — knowledge graph, notes, email index — stays on your local machine.

How does Rowboat compare to Obsidian’s AI plugins?#

Rowboat and Obsidian serve different purposes. Obsidian is a note-taking app with AI plugins; Rowboat is an AI coworker with a notes surface. Rowboat’s key advantage is that its knowledge graph is built automatically from your email, meetings, and browser activity, not just from notes you manually write. However, Rowboat’s notes are Obsidian-compatible, so you can use both tools together.

Does Rowboat support Microsoft Outlook or other email providers?#

Currently, Rowboat’s email surface supports Gmail only. Support for other providers like Outlook, ProtonMail, and custom IMAP is not yet available. The project’s active development pace suggests this may change in future releases, but there is no confirmed timeline.

What hardware do I need to run local models with Rowboat?#

For local models in the 7B-13B parameter range, a consumer GPU with 8-12GB VRAM is recommended. For larger models (30B+), 24GB+ VRAM is needed. Rowboat also works with CPU-only inference through Ollama, though performance will be significantly slower. Users without powerful hardware can use hosted API keys while keeping their knowledge graph local.
