Palmier Pro Review: Open-Source macOS AI Video Editor That Lets Claude Edit Your Timeline

Palmier Pro is a free, open-source macOS video editor with AI generation built into the timeline and MCP support for agent-driven editing via Claude or Cursor.

Palmier Pro is a free, open-source macOS video editor that embeds AI generation directly into the timeline and exposes the entire editing environment as an MCP (Model Context Protocol) server, allowing AI agents like Claude, Codex, and Cursor to read, edit, and assemble sequences programmatically. Built by a Y Combinator-backed two-person team and released in June 2026, it has already gathered over 12,500 GitHub stars by solving a problem every AI content creator knows: the tedious loop of generating clips in one tool, downloading them, and manually arranging them in a traditional NLE.

What Is Palmier Pro? — The One-Sentence Pitch#

Palmier Pro is a native macOS video editor (macOS 26, Apple Silicon only) whose editor core is fully free and open source under GPLv3, while AI generation runs on a paid credit system. What makes it genuinely different from every other AI video tool is its MCP architecture: instead of bolting a chatbot onto the side of a video editor, Palmier Pro turns the entire timeline into a tool that AI agents can operate directly. Claude can add clips, trim segments, apply transitions, and generate new footage — all without you touching the mouse.

The Architecture That Makes It Different — MCP Timeline Explained#

The Model Context Protocol (MCP) is an open standard originally popularized by Anthropic for connecting AI models to external tools. Palmier Pro runs an MCP server at http://127.0.0.1:19789/mcp per project. When you connect Claude Desktop, Cursor, or Codex to this server, the agent gains read and write access to your timeline as a set of tools.

This means an AI agent can:

  • Read the current timeline structure — clips, durations, order
  • Add new clips from local files or generated footage
  • Trim and rearrange existing clips
  • Generate new video segments using any connected AI model
  • Export the final sequence to MP4, ProRes, or NLE XML

The architecture is genuinely novel. Most “AI video editors” on the market are wrappers around text-to-video APIs with a chat interface bolted on. Palmier Pro flips the model: the editor is a capable multi-track NLE first, and the AI integration is a protocol that lets agents drive it. This is the difference between a tool that has AI features and a tool that AI can use.

Key Features Deep Dive#

On-Timeline AI Generation#

The headline feature is generating video clips directly onto the timeline without leaving the editor. Select a gap or placeholder clip, describe what you want, and the generation engine produces footage that lands directly in the sequence. This eliminates the generate-download-import-rearrange loop that costs content creators hours per video.

A single credit balance covers multiple frontier models. You are not locked into one provider. The same 5,000-credit monthly pool works across Seedance 2.0, Kling V3, Veo 3.1, Grok Imagine, and Nano Banana Pro. This is a significant advantage over tools that force you to buy separate subscriptions for each model.

Agent-Driven Editing via MCP#

This is Palmier Pro’s killer feature and the reason it has attracted attention far beyond its feature set. When an AI agent like Claude connects to the Palmier MCP server, it can perform real editing operations on the timeline.

For a content pipeline operator, this unlocks a fully automated workflow:

  1. An agent writes a script
  2. A generation agent produces footage clips
  3. The same agent (or another) arranges them on the Palmier timeline
  4. The agent trims, orders, and exports the final video
  5. The result is ready for upload

No human needs to touch a video editor for routine content. This is the closest thing to a fully autonomous video production pipeline available today.

Export Options and Workflow Integration#

Palmier Pro exports to standard formats: MP4 (H.264, H.265) and Apple ProRes for final delivery, plus NLE XML for Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve. The XML export is critical for professionals who need to hand off to a colorist or add motion graphics in a more mature tool.

The editor also includes local transcription (on-device, no data leaves your machine) and smart search across your media library — both free features that don’t require an account.

The free tier is genuinely useful — you get the full multi-track NLE, MCP integration, local transcription, and smart search without paying anything or even creating an account.

The launch pricing is temporary. Pro goes from $29 to $49, and Max from $69 to $99 once the introductory period ends. This is a common YC playbook: low prices to build a user base, then gradual increases as the product matures.

The Good — What Palmier Pro Gets Right#

Genuinely Free, Capable Editor#

The free tier is not a crippled demo. You get the full multi-track timeline, MCP server, local transcription, and smart search. No account required, no watermarks, no time limits. This is rare in the AI video space, where most “free” tools limit exports to 720p or add watermarks.

Kills the Generate-Download-Import Loop#

Every AI content creator knows the pain: generate a clip in Runway or Kling, download the MP4, open Premiere, import the file, find the right spot on the timeline, drop it in. Repeat 20 times per video. Palmier Pro eliminates this entirely. Generation output lands directly on the timeline at the cursor position.

Novel Agent-Operates-App Architecture#

This is the architectural insight that makes Palmier Pro more than just another AI video tool. By exposing the editor as an MCP server, the founders have created a platform that improves as AI agents get smarter. When Claude or Codex gains new capabilities, Palmier Pro benefits automatically — no update required.

Transparent Open-Source Core (GPLv3)#

The editor core is fully open source. Anyone can inspect the code, audit for privacy concerns, fork it, or contribute. The generation pipeline is closed source (this is where the company makes money), but the editing foundation is transparent. For privacy-conscious creators, this is a meaningful advantage over black-box competitors.

The Bad — What’s Missing or Limiting#

macOS 26 / Apple Silicon Only#

This is the single biggest limitation. Palmier Pro requires macOS 26 (Tahoe) on Apple Silicon. No Windows, no Linux, no Intel Macs, no web version. If you are on a PC or an older Mac, you cannot use it at all. The founders have stated this is a deliberate choice for native Swift performance, but it dramatically limits the addressable market.

Pre-1.0 Maturity (v0.6.8)#

As of July 2026, Palmier Pro is at version 0.6.8. It is functional but clearly early-stage. Users report occasional crashes, unfinished UI elements, and missing polish. This is expected for a pre-1.0 product, but it means production workflows should have a fallback plan.

No Effects, Transitions, Color Grading, or Graphics#

The feature gap is significant. Palmier Pro has no:

  • Video effects or filters
  • Transition library (beyond basic cuts)
  • Color grading tools
  • Masking or rotoscoping
  • Motion graphics or text animation
  • Keyframing

For professional video work, you will still need to export XML to DaVinci Resolve or Premiere Pro for finishing. Palmier Pro is a generation and assembly tool, not a full post-production suite.

No Distribution or Publishing#

There is no built-in publishing to YouTube, TikTok, or social platforms. You export an MP4 and upload manually. This is consistent with the tool’s philosophy (it is an editor, not a platform), but it means the “fully automated pipeline” still has a manual upload step at the end.

Credit Costs Not Transparent Per-Generation#

Palmier Pro does not publish how many credits each generation consumes. A simple text-to-video might cost 50 credits, while a complex multi-scene generation might cost 500. Without per-generation pricing, it is difficult to estimate the real cost of a project before you run it. Users report that 5,000 credits can produce 3–7 minutes of video, but the variance is wide depending on model choice and prompt complexity.

Who Is Palmier Pro For?#

Palmier Pro is ideal for three groups:

  1. Mac developers using Claude Code or Cursor — If you already work with AI coding agents, Palmier Pro extends that workflow to video. Your agent can write the script, generate footage, and assemble the video in one session.

  2. Content pipeline operators — If you produce regular video content (YouTube, social media, marketing) and want to automate as much of the pipeline as possible, Palmier Pro’s MCP architecture lets you build autonomous production workflows.

  3. AI video experimenters on Mac — If you have a modern Mac and want to explore AI video generation without committing to a subscription, the free tier gives you the full editor and MCP integration at zero cost.

Who Should Skip It?#

Skip Palmier Pro if:

  • You are on Windows or Linux — There is no version for you, and none has been announced.
  • You need professional finishing tools — If your work requires color grading, motion graphics, or effects, Palmier Pro cannot replace your current NLE.
  • You need a web-based tool — Palmier Pro is a native app only. No browser version exists.
  • You want predictable per-video costs — The opaque credit system makes it hard to budget for production.

Verdict — Is It Worth It?#

Palmier Pro earns a strong recommendation with important caveats. For its target audience — Mac-based developers and content pipeline operators — it is the most innovative video editing tool released in 2026. The MCP architecture is not a gimmick; it is a genuinely new way to think about how AI agents interact with creative tools.

The free tier is excellent and costs nothing to try. If you have a Mac on macOS 26, download it, connect Claude, and see what an agent-driven timeline feels like. For the price of a few coffees, the Pro plan at $29/mo gives you enough credits to produce regular content.

The limitations are real but not fatal. The macOS-only restriction is the hardest constraint. The missing effects and grading tools mean Palmier Pro is not a Premiere or DaVinci replacement — yet. But as a generation and assembly tool that AI agents can drive directly, nothing else on the market comes close.

Score: 7.5/10 — Innovative architecture, excellent free tier, but early-stage maturity and platform limitations keep it from being a universal recommendation.

FAQ#

Is Palmier Pro really free?#

Yes. The editor core, MCP server, local transcription, and smart search are completely free with no account required, no watermarks, and no time limits. You only pay for AI generation credits.

Does Palmier Pro work on Windows or Linux?#

No. Palmier Pro requires macOS 26 (Tahoe) on Apple Silicon. There is no Windows, Linux, Intel Mac, or web version available or announced.

What AI models does Palmier Pro support?#

Palmier Pro supports Seedance 2.0, Kling V3, Veo 3.1, Grok Imagine, and Nano Banana Pro through a unified credit system. You can switch between models freely within your monthly credit pool.

Can Palmier Pro replace Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve?#

Not yet. Palmier Pro lacks effects, transitions, color grading, masking, and motion graphics. It excels at AI generation and timeline assembly. For professional finishing, export XML to Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve.

How do I connect Claude to Palmier Pro?#

Palmier Pro runs an MCP server at http://127.0.0.1:19789/mcp per project. Connect Claude Desktop, Cursor, or Codex to this endpoint, and the agent gains read/write access to your timeline as a set of tools.
