What Is Inkling and Why Does It Matter?#

Inkling is the first in-house model from Thinking Machines Lab, the AI company founded by former OpenAI CTO Mira Murati. Released on July 15, 2026, Inkling is a 975-billion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) transformer with 41 billion active parameters per token, released under the Apache 2.0 license with fully open weights. It represents the first time a major US AI lab has released a truly competitive open-weight model that can stand toe-to-toe with leading Chinese open models like DeepSeek V4 and Kimi K2.6, scoring an Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index of 41 — the highest among US open-weights models.

What Is the Inkling Architecture and How Does It Work?#

Inkling is built on a 66-layer decoder-only transformer with a sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) feed-forward backbone. The model routes each input token to 6 out of 256 expert networks, plus 2 shared experts that activate on every token. This design means only 41 billion of the 975 billion total parameters are active for any single forward pass, dramatically reducing computational cost while maintaining the knowledge capacity of a much larger model.

The architecture incorporates hybrid local and global attention layers, allowing the model to efficiently process both short-range and long-range dependencies. This hybrid attention design is critical for Inkling’s 1-million-token context window — one of the largest among open-weight models. On the Tinker API, the context window is capped at 256K tokens, but the full model supports up to 1 million tokens for self-hosted deployments.

Inkling was pretrained on 45 trillion tokens spanning text, images, audio, and video, making it a natively multimodal model rather than a text-only model with separate vision or audio adapters bolted on later. This native multimodality is a key differentiator among open-weight models, as most competitors (Nemotron 3 Ultra, DeepSeek V4, Llama 4) either lack native multimodal support or handle non-text modalities through separate pipelines.

The model’s safety benchmarks are also noteworthy. Inkling scores 78.0% on the FORTRESS Adversarial benchmark and 98.6% on StrongREJECT, indicating robust resistance to adversarial attacks and jailbreak attempts — an important consideration for enterprise deployments.

How Does Inkling’s Token Efficiency Compare to Competitors?#

One of Inkling’s most compelling advantages is token efficiency. According to Artificial Analysis, Inkling uses an average of 25,000 output tokens per Intelligence Index task, compared to 43,000 for GLM-5.2, 38,000 for Kimi K2.6, and 37,000 for DeepSeek V4 Pro. This represents roughly 40% fewer tokens than the competition for equivalent task performance.

Token efficiency translates directly into cost savings. At Inkling’s pricing of $1.87 per million input tokens and $4.68 per million output tokens (64K context), the lower token consumption means effective per-task costs are significantly lower than competitors that require more tokens to produce the same quality of output.

The TechCrunch review also noted that Inkling uses one-third as many tokens as Nemotron 3 Ultra for the same coding performance, further reinforcing the efficiency narrative. This efficiency is likely a product of the MoE architecture’s ability to route tokens to specialized experts, combined with the hybrid attention mechanism that reduces redundant processing.

What Multimodal Capabilities Does Inkling Offer?#

Inkling is natively multimodal, trained from the ground up on text, images, audio, and video. This is a significant differentiator in the open-weight space, where most models are either text-only or handle non-text modalities through separate, less integrated pipelines.

The native multimodality means Inkling can process and reason across different media types in a single forward pass, without needing separate encoders or adapters for each modality. This unified approach typically leads to better cross-modal reasoning — for example, understanding how a diagram relates to a textual description, or how audio instructions map to visual outputs.

Among open-weight models, this native multimodal capability is rare. DeepSeek V4 has some multimodal support but through separate components. Nemotron 3 Ultra is primarily text-focused. Llama 4 added vision but not audio. Inkling’s comprehensive multimodal support positions it as the most versatile open-weight model for applications that need to process diverse data types.

What Is Thinking Machines’ Open-Weight Strategy?#

Thinking Machines Lab’s open-weight strategy is a direct challenge to the “one-size-fits-all” approach of proprietary AI models. Mira Murati has positioned Inkling as the “anti-OpenAI” bet — instead of offering a single, black-box API that organizations must adapt their workflows around, Thinking Machines provides the model weights under Apache 2.0 so organizations can download, inspect, modify, and fine-tune the model for their specific needs.

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella articulated a compelling argument for this approach, warning that enterprises using proprietary AI effectively “pay twice” — once through subscription fees and again by feeding their proprietary business knowledge into a model they don’t control. Open-weight models like Inkling eliminate the second cost: organizations own their customizations and their data remains within their infrastructure.

Thinking Machines is not claiming Inkling is the best model on every metric. Instead, the company positions it as a well-rounded starting point for customization. The Tinker platform enables organizations to fine-tune Inkling for their specific use cases, and Thinking Machines explicitly states that customers are responsible for the safety of their customizations — a pragmatic stance that shifts accountability while enabling maximum flexibility.

What Is the Tinker Platform and How Does Self-Fine-Tuning Work?#

Tinker is Thinking Machines Lab’s fine-tuning and deployment platform, designed to make Inkling customization accessible to organizations without deep ML expertise. In a striking demonstration of the model’s capabilities, Thinking Machines showed Inkling writing its own fine-tuning job on Tinker — the model analyzed its own performance gaps, wrote the fine-tuning script, and executed the training run autonomously.

This self-fine-tuning capability hints at a future where models can continuously improve themselves with minimal human intervention. For enterprise users, this means deploying Inkling and then letting it adapt to their specific domain data, terminology, and workflows through an automated process rather than requiring a team of ML engineers to manage the fine-tuning pipeline.

The Tinker API currently supports a 256K token context window, and the platform handles the infrastructure for distributed fine-tuning across multiple GPUs. This lowers the barrier to entry for organizations that want customized AI but lack the hardware to run the full 2TB VRAM checkpoint.

What Are the Hardware Requirements for Running Inkling?#

The hardware requirements for Inkling are substantial and represent one of the most significant barriers to self-hosted deployment. The BF16 checkpoint requires approximately 2 terabytes of VRAM, which translates to 8 NVIDIA B300 GPUs or 16 H200 GPUs. This is enterprise-grade infrastructure that puts full self-hosting out of reach for most small and medium organizations.

For organizations with more modest hardware budgets, Thinking Machines offers an NVFP4 (W4A4) quantized checkpoint that reduces VRAM requirements to approximately 600GB — achievable with 4 B300 GPUs or 8 H200 GPUs. This 4-bit quantization represents a 3.3x memory reduction while maintaining most of the model’s capabilities.

Inkling is supported by major inference frameworks including SGLang, vLLM, TokenSpeed, Unsloth, and Hugging Face Transformers, giving organizations flexibility in their deployment stack. The wide framework support means teams can use their existing inference infrastructure rather than being locked into a proprietary runtime.

For organizations that cannot justify the hardware investment, the Tinker API provides access to Inkling at $1.87 per million input tokens without any upfront hardware costs. This pay-per-use model makes Inkling accessible to teams of all sizes, even if only large enterprises can self-host.

What Enterprise Use Cases Does Inkling Enable?#

The Bridgewater Associates partnership serves as the flagship enterprise proof of concept for Inkling. Bridgewater, one of the world’s largest hedge funds, worked with Thinking Machines to customize Inkling for their specific financial analysis workflows. This demonstrates that Inkling can be adapted for domain-specific applications requiring high accuracy, data privacy, and regulatory compliance.

Enterprise use cases well-suited to Inkling include:

  • Financial analysis and reporting: Custom fine-tuning on proprietary financial data with strict data residency requirements
  • Legal document processing: Apache 2.0 license allows full customization for legal terminology and jurisdiction-specific knowledge
  • Healthcare and life sciences: On-premise deployment ensures HIPAA compliance while leveraging Inkling’s multimodal capabilities for medical imaging and clinical notes
  • Customer support systems: Token efficiency and caching make high-volume customer interactions cost-effective
  • Code generation and software engineering: SWEBench Verified score of 77.6% demonstrates strong coding capabilities suitable for internal developer tools

The self-fine-tuning capability is particularly valuable for enterprises that want to deploy AI but lack the ML engineering headcount to manage complex fine-tuning pipelines. Inkling can analyze its own performance on enterprise data and write its own fine-tuning jobs, dramatically reducing the expertise required to achieve high-quality custom results.

What Are the Limitations and Concerns with Inkling?#

Despite its impressive capabilities, Inkling has several limitations worth considering:

Hardware barrier: The 2TB VRAM requirement for the BF16 checkpoint limits self-hosting to organizations with substantial GPU infrastructure. While the NVFP4 quantization reduces this to 600GB, even that requires multiple high-end GPUs.

New ecosystem: As a first-generation model from a new company, Inkling’s ecosystem of tools, community resources, and third-party integrations is less mature than what exists for Llama 4 or DeepSeek V4. Organizations may encounter fewer pre-built solutions and community fine-tunes.

Safety responsibility: Thinking Machines explicitly states that customers are responsible for the safety of their customizations. Organizations that fine-tune Inkling must have their own safety evaluation pipelines, which adds complexity to the deployment process.

Not best-in-class on every metric: Thinking Machines has been transparent that Inkling is not the top performer on every benchmark. While it leads in agentic performance and token efficiency, some specialized models may outperform it on specific tasks.

Tinker API context limit: The 256K token context window on the Tinker API is significantly smaller than the model’s native 1M token capability, which may be a limitation for organizations that need the full context window but cannot self-host.

Frequently Asked Questions#

What is Inkling and who created it?#

Inkling is a 975-billion-parameter open-weight language model created by Thinking Machines Lab, the AI company founded by former OpenAI CTO Mira Murati. It was released on July 15, 2026 under the Apache 2.0 license and is the company’s first in-house model.

How many active parameters does Inkling use per token?#

Inkling uses 41 billion active parameters per token through its Mixture-of-Experts architecture. The model has 975 billion total parameters but routes each token to only 6 of 256 expert networks plus 2 shared experts, keeping computational costs manageable.

Can I run Inkling on my own hardware?#

Yes, but the hardware requirements are substantial. The full BF16 checkpoint requires 2TB of VRAM (8x B300 or 16x H200 GPUs). A quantized NVFP4 checkpoint reduces this to 600GB VRAM (4x B300 or 8x H200). For those without such hardware, the Tinker API provides cloud access at $1.87 per million input tokens.

How does Inkling compare to DeepSeek V4 and Nemotron 3 Ultra?#

Inkling achieves an Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index of 41, beating Nemotron 3 Ultra (38) and competing closely with DeepSeek V4. It leads in agentic performance (GDPval-AA Elo 1238) and token efficiency (25K vs 37K average output tokens), and is the only major open-weight model with native multimodal support for text, images, audio, and video.

What license does Inkling use and can I use it commercially?#

Inkling is released under the Apache 2.0 license, one of the most permissive open-source licenses. This allows commercial use, modification, distribution, and sublicensing without royalty payments, making it suitable for enterprise applications that require full control over their AI infrastructure.
